## Findings

**F1. Minor. The module grids are bottom-edge-bound at the final checkpoint; the asymmetry with M-aff's five-rate grid is real but worth at most 0.003 nats to D1.**
Evidence: `05-spreads.json anchors[*].harm_inventory` (dev loss per candidate). At the 3,200-step checkpoint M-aff's dev loss is monotone decreasing to its floor 0.01 on all six anchors (0.1 minus 0.01: 0.014, 0.013, 0.015, 0.021, 0.018, 0.018 nats), and M-sq's is monotone decreasing to its floor 0.1 on all six (likewise M-wide, M-small, M-small-B); M-sq selected 0.1 on four anchors, M-aff selected 0.03 or 0.01 on four. But the checkpoint axis compensates: M-aff's selected candidate's dev loss minus the best dev candidate among its rates ≥ 0.1 (any checkpoint) is −0.001, +0.001, +0.002, 0.000, 0.000, +0.003 (anchors 3000 to 3005), because 0.1@800 reaches what 0.03@3200 reaches.
Reasoning: rate × steps is the effective regulariser on seen data, so the grid's rate floor is not binding once early checkpoints are selectable; the two extra M-aff rates (§4, meant to protect the control) gave it at most one per-example SE over what the module grid would have given. D1's estimand is therefore matched to within about 0.003 nats, far below X = 0.02, and the direction (against M-sq, conservative for "present", slightly anti-conservative for "equivalent") cannot change a reading. The `edge_of_grid` flags overstate the issue for the same reason.
Confidence: high on the bound for M-aff; medium that M-sq's response to lower rates would be as small (inferred from the control).
Action: none before the confirmatory; report the bound beside the edge-of-grid counts. X must not move: D1 has been seen.

**F2. Major (no verdict role). The h ∈ {16, 4, 2} refit survival points are an artefact of the edge-of-grid M-wide rate on three anchors.**
Evidence: on 3003, 3004, 3005 M-wide selected rate 1.0 at 200 steps (`modules-3003/result.json payload.arms["M-wide"].report.best`: rate 1.0, steps 200, selection loss 0.5253; the same grid's 1.0@800 is 0.5553 and 1.0@3200 is 0.709). Amendment 3 and §5 fix the h ≠ 8 refits at "the M-wide rate, 800 steps", so those refits ran at 1.0 for 800 steps. `M-wide→16:refit` on 3003: `step0_selection_loss 0.5284`, after refit `selection_loss 0.5527`; dev gain 0.009 against the as-sliced 0.029. Refit survival at h = 16: 0.26, −0.02, 0.44 on exactly those anchors versus 1.07, 0.97, 1.32 on the others (`quantities.survival.refit["16"]`). h = 8 refit runs the full grid and is unaffected, so D2 is clean.
Reasoning: the fixed 800-step refit inherits the selected rate but not the selected step count; where selection picked an early-stopped high-rate candidate, the refit overshoots and destroys the warm start. The curve mixes two procedures (full grid at h = 8, one run elsewhere).
Confidence: high. Risk: low; A3.1 put these arms in the confirmatory set with bounds.
Action: before the confirmatory spends compute on them, carry the selected step count with the rate, or run the module grid at every h; else label the refit curve mixed-procedure.

**F3. Major (design adequacy, for the read). V2 at n = 32 is most likely to return "indeterminate".**
Evidence: pilot D2 per anchor 0.0092, 0.0057, −0.0021, −0.0021, −0.0022, 0.0051 (median 0.0015, SD 0.0050, `05-spreads.json sd.D2`); X₂ = 0.01, "hurts" at 0. Forward run of the preregistered rule with the implemented percentile bootstrap of the median at n = 32 (CPU, 1,500 replications, normal draws): true D2 = 0.0015 gives indeterminate 0.999; 0.005 gives 1.000; "helps" needs a true D2 of about 0.013 (0.85); "hurts" needs about −0.003 (0.87).
Reasoning: this is the design working as frozen (X₂ was set at "three half-widths"), not a defect; but the pack's rule is to run the criteria forward before spending the budget, and the most likely output is the uninformative one. Confidence: high. Action: none required; John should read the confirmatory go knowing V2's expected outcome.

**F4. Minor. The V1 certification gate carries about a 7 percent chance of voiding V1 at n = 32.**

**F5. Minor. The achieved half-widths in `06-confirmatory-count.json` are the six-anchor widths, not projections to n = 32, and D1's projected width is not published.**

**F6. Observation. The 1.25 inflation in `anchor_count` slightly understates the bootstrap-median width.** n* = ceil((1.645 × 1.25 × sd / (X/2))²); at n = 32 the implemented percentile-bootstrap median bound is 1.46 × the normal mean bound. Immaterial to the count (F7). Confidence: high.

**F7. Observation (clean). n* = 1 is the correct output of the preregistered rule and the floor binds robustly.**
Evidence: `sd_d1 0.0032761` reproduced from the six `quantities.D1` values (n − 1); (1.645 × 1.25 × 0.003276 / 0.01)² = 0.45, ceil 1; `n_star 1`, `confirmatory_count 32`. A six-value SD has a 95 percent interval of 0.62 × to 2.45 × (chi-square, 5 df), so SD(D1) ≤ 0.0080 and n* ≤ 3 under any plausible value. Confidence: high.

**F8. Observation (clean). Pairing, units and data roles are as promised.**
Evidence: unit is the anchor seed; every gain is against the anchor's own N0 (`harm_baseline` on all 25 arms, all anchors); D3 against the T-cool host's own N0 (`03-hosts.json tcool_noop` R-bn on all six); S-static after its own no-op rule (`static_noop`, amendment 4). `fit.choose` selects by `selection_loss` alone; the harm inventory reads dev but filters nothing, and selected candidates differ from the dev-best on several anchors (3002 M-wide: selected 0.1@3200, dev-best 0.3@800). SDs are over six per-anchor values; the per-example SEs (`*_se`, 10,000 resamples of 5,000 dev examples) are beside and read by no verdict. Confirmatory seeds are fresh; `07-development-medians.json` is flagged `development_only`. `confirmatory.read_once` publishes median, 5 and 95 percent anchor-bootstrap order statistics and the exact sign-test bound for every verdict and beside quantity including each survival width (A3.2); verdicts read `anchor_bound`; at n = 32 the sign rank is 11 (one-sided coverage 0.975, conservative); at n = 6 the sign bound is the sample extreme, which is why the pilot reads no median. Confidence: high.

**F9. Observation. **F10. Observation. D1′ takes a max over two dev gains** (`stats.quantities`): selection on report data, but in the conservative direction for the "beats every named re-tune" label. Pilot D1′ median 0.003, positive on 5 of 6. Confidence: high.

## Questions A to D

**A. Sizing.** n* = 1 is the credible, reproduced output of §9 step 6 (F7). It is not a sign of mis-specification; it is the consequence of SD(D1) being 0.0033 where J1 planned X = 0.02 as "the smallest margin resolvable at n = 32", which under `anchor_count` means a planning SD of 0.0275 (the same constant §7 uses for S-static admission). The pilot SD is 8.4 × smaller, so X is now a substantive decision threshold, not a resolvability limit; it stays frozen because D1 has been seen. Power at n = 32 (forward run of the implemented rules, F3 method): V1 reads "equivalent" with probability 1.00 for true D1 ≤ 0.01 and 0.99 at 0.017, "present" 0.99 at 0.023, and is indeterminate only in the band 0.018 to 0.022 (0.90 at exactly 0.020); with SD inflated 2.45 × the band widens to about 0.015 to 0.025. D3 reads "harvests undertraining" with probability 1.00 for any true D3 below about 0.017 (pilot median −0.0014, SD 0.0021). D5 gives no reading at the pilot median 0.013 and needs a true D5 near 0.028 for a 0.7 chance of "design-in beats insertion". V2: F3. Certification gates: F4. From six anchors one can conclude that 32 fresh anchors will resolve V1 and D3 decisively and will not resolve V2 unless the true D2 sits outside (−0.003, 0.013); one cannot conclude anything about the medians themselves (the six-anchor sign bound is the sample extreme), nor pin any SD better than a factor of about 2.5.

**B. Paired comparisons and uncertainty.** Correct as promised (F8). Selection bias: candidate choice is on the selection set and reporting on dev, so no winner's curse enters the dev numbers; D1′ is the one max on report data and it is conservative (F10). Double use: none across the pilot and confirmatory (fresh seeds); within the pilot the development anchors serve sizing, admission and the schedule rule by design (§9 "what the pilot may fill"). Edge-of-grid contamination: not as bias. It distorts the h ∈ {16, 4, 2} refit survival points directly (F2); its effect on D1 is bounded at about 0.003 nats (F1); D2 and the h = 8 refits are clean (full grid). The survival widths are published with bounds and sign test per A3.2.

**C. Instrument.** Expected under the design: the parameter-step ratio 4.3 on every anchor (`d2_parameter_step_ratio`: final-checkpoint grid bookings, a §4 disclosure with zero variance); survival ratios above 1 (the h = 8 refit spends a second full grid; 1.04 to 1.22) and below 0 (sliced h = 2 is worse than N0 on 3000, 3001; the ratio is of unbounded gains, undefined by A3.2 only when G(M-wide) ≤ 0); D5_norefit negative with D5 positive (the unrefit static module never collects the 0.03 to 0.05 nat recalibration-and-finishing gain that every refit arm, R-cal and R-aff collect; the refit comparison is the matched one); the uncertified anchor 3004 (0.83 ≥ 0.75, no raise). Warrants an amendment or John's recorded acceptance before the confirmatory: F2 only. V0 passed on every recorded flag; fold residuals within 2.5e-4 of 0 against the 0.005 tolerance.
