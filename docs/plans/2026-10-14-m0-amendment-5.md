## What the pilot reviews found

The M0 pilot ran on 2026-10-14 from `main` 0d4179a under identity
`m0-v4.2` and finished pilot-clean (evidence
`docs/results/m0-pilot-2026-10-14-0d4179a/`). Three independent reviews
of its receipts (`docs/reviews/m0-capacity/pilot/`: statistics,
determinism and replay, training dynamics) found nothing blocking and
three majors:

- **Survival refit** (statistics F2, training dynamics F1). §5 line 292
  gives M-wide→{16, 4, 2} "one refit at the M-wide rate, 800 steps". The
  module grid is a flat rate-by-steps ridge, so a rate is only meaningful
  with its step count: on the three anchors where M-wide selected rate 1.0
  at 200 steps, 800 steps at that rate overtrained the warm start and the
  h = 16 refit ended worse than its sliced start (selection loss 0.5527,
  0.5826, 0.5960 against step-0 0.5284, 0.5379, 0.5553 on seeds 3003 to
  3005), making the refit survival curve non-monotone in h on half the
  anchors. No verdict reads the survival widths, but amendment 3 put them
  in the confirmatory set.
- **The no-op host's hash is single-sourced** (determinism F1). The
  BatchNorm-recalibrated N0 host that every later arm loads is hashed
  once; nothing replays the recalibration on the other card.
- **Environment identity is incomplete** (determinism F2): Python, driver,
  cuDNN and the GPU model are not in the recorded environment.

The minors are receipt additions (determinism F3 to F5, statistics F5,
training dynamics F3 with statistics F1) or items to report (below).

## A5.1 The survival refit carries the selected (rate, step) pair (§5 line 292; amendment 3 A3.1)

M-wide→h for h ∈ {16, 4, 2} is refit **once, at M-wide's selected rate,
for M-wide's selected step count** (the rate and step of the module grid's
chosen candidate on the anchor's `checkpoints` schedule), warm-started
from the sliced tensors with fold, as before. The refit's step count is
published beside its rate and its step-0 selection loss. The fixed 800
steps are retired.

Booking (replaces the A3.1 nominal): each refit is booked at the module
grid's measured per-step kernel times the **last checkpoint** (the bound
on the selected step count, 3,200 steps on the default schedule) plus one
development evaluation, about 14 s per refit nominal and 42 s per anchor.
The measured per-anchor charge the confirmatory launcher carries from the
pilot (ruling I3) was taken with 800-step refits; the launcher raises it
by three refits times (last checkpoint − 800) steps at the pilot's
measured per-step module kernel (the "survival refit uplift", about 32 s
per anchor, about 1,000 s at n = 32) before testing the ceiling, and
publishes the uplift. The pilot's refit survival values stand as published
and are labelled an instrument artefact on the anchors where M-wide
selected 1.0 at 200 steps; nothing is re-derived.

Unchanged: the M-wide→8 refits (the full module grid, §5 line 291), the
sliced cuts, the fold, the survival ratio definition, the arm set of §10
as amended by amendment 3, and the no-verdict role of every survival
width.

The pilot of record ran under identity `m0-v4.2` (admission certificate 3)
and is not re-run: the confirmatory launcher admits a clean pilot result
recorded under `m0-v4.2` or under this identity, publishes which, and
takes the pilot's measured kernels as they stand, with the uplift above
applied when the pilot's refit kernel was the 800-step one. The
confirmatory's implementation source differs from the pilot's, so §10's
hash-listed source-change disposition in the admission certificate names
the admitted source, as before (rulings R27, R28).

## A5.2 Reporting additions (receipts only; no rule, margin, arm, seed or ceiling changes)

1. **Environment identity** (determinism F2). Beside the five pinned
   fields (torch wheel and version, CUDA, numpy wheel and version, which
   still refuse a mismatch), the environment record carries the Python
   version, the NVIDIA driver version, the cuDNN version and the GPU
   names. The new fields are recorded, not pinned: the confirmatory
   publishes, field by field, whether they equal the pilot's record, and
   never refuses on them, so a routine driver update is a published
   difference rather than a stop.
2. **No-op receipts** (determinism F1, F4). Every no-op rule application
   (the anchor's N0 and T-cool's) publishes the SHA-256 of the fit-index
   tensor the recalibration consumed and both selection losses that
   decided the choice.
3. **Cross-card no-op replay** (determinism F1). The confirmatory V0 runs
   the attempt-4 anchor's no-op child on both cards after the replay and
   holds as an instrument failure unless the raw, recalibrated and chosen
   state hashes agree bitwise. It is charged inside the fixed V0 term.
4. **Terminal records** (determinism F3). `pilot-result.json` and
   `confirmatory-result.json` carry the admission certificate's SHA-256
   and the custody path.
5. **Child environment and exit** (determinism F5). Every worker receipt
   records the determinism-relevant environment variables it ran under
   (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES`, `CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS`,
   `MKL_NUM_THREADS`, `PYTHONHASHSEED`, `CUDA_LAUNCH_BLOCKING`,
   `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF`, `TORCH_CUDNN_V8_API_DISABLED`, absent ones
   named absent), and the M0 supervisor publishes each child's return code
   and charge beside its process record. The shared Gate 3 process record
   is byte-unchanged.
6. **Projected half-widths** (statistics F5). The stage-6 count publishes,
   beside the six-anchor achieved half-widths, the half-width each of D1,
   D2, D3 and D5 projects to at the confirmatory count under the same
   anchor bootstrap, labelled a projection from development anchors.
7. **Grid corners** (training dynamics F3, statistics F1). Beside the
   preregistered edge-of-grid flag, the spreads receipt publishes each
   grid arm's selected (rate, step) corner on every anchor (rate at the
   grid's lowest, highest or an interior value; step at the first, last or
   an interior checkpoint) and the per-arm counts of each corner.

## Reported, not changed

The reviewers' remaining findings are read items. They are recorded here
so the gate C read and the confirmatory go are taken knowing them, and
none moves a frozen value: X stays at 0.02 nats because D1 has been seen
(statistics F1).

- A forward run of the preregistered V2 rule at n = 32 on the pilot's D2
  (median 0.0015, SD 0.005) returns "indeterminate" with probability 0.999;
  "helps" needs a true D2 near 0.013 (statistics F3).
- The M-aff 75 percent certification gate carries about a 7 percent chance
  of voiding V1 at n = 32 at the pilot's 5-of-6 rate (statistics F4);
  M-aff's paired SE is tighter than the module arms', so its certification
  bar is about 0.0013 nats (training dynamics F5).
- Every anchor is scored on the same 5,000 development examples; a claim
  about CIFAR-10 at large would add the per-example SE (statistics F9).
- M-sq | T-cool selects the coldest grid corner (0.1 at 200 steps) on all
  six anchors; the bias on D3 is a few millinats toward zero against
  X = 0.02 (training dynamics F2). R-272s sits at the top of the re-tune
  grid (F7). The module grids are bottom-edge-bound at the final
  checkpoint but checkpoint selection compensates (statistics F1).
- The CPU slicing known answer records no CPU determinism context
  (determinism F6).
